:::foreword
You draw, and the site holds the prompt. The image is a reading of your sketch under a brief you see only afterward.
:::

Most image UIs put the prompt in the foreground and treat drawing as an advanced mode. Sketch generation reverses that order. The reader’s mark is the input on screen. The site chooses the prompt and keeps it back until the image exists, so the sketch carries the intent and the brief only steers the render.

The contract is select-then-generate, not chat. Behind the pad sits a small closed set of prompts: still life under soft light, portrait against a plain field, interior with a single window. The interface picks one, or the reader picks from short labels that do not reveal the full text. Generation runs only when there is a mark on the pad. An empty pad stays quiet, and wait chrome belongs on the generate path alone.

When the image lands, attribution stays visible. The sketch remains beside the result, and the prompt appears after the fact as provenance, the way a caption names the model. That order matters. If the full prompt were editable up front, the sketch would become decoration. Here the sketch is the ask, and the hidden brief is a constraint the site applies, not a second conversation.

The pattern fails in two ways: the brief invents detail the sketch never offered, or the pad is only a stamp over a text prompt. Restraint means the render follows the marks that are there. Iteration can clear the pad, keep the same brief, or swap the brief without rewriting a paragraph. Stopping is simpler. Leave the last sketch and its image on screen, side by side, a drawing with its caption.

:::notes
The prompt set is closed and site-authored. Generation waits for a mark. Empty pad is silence.
:::
